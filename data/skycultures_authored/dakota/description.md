# D(L)akota

## Introduction

D(L)akota names for constellations and stars, as published in the
open-access scholarly record of the **Native Skywatchers** initiative — a
revitalization programme founded by Dr. Annette S. Lee, working with
D(L)akota language experts, artists, elders and educators.

What is recorded here is **names and their translations only**. No outlines
are drawn, and no artwork is reproduced. See *Constellations* below for why.

## Description

Native Skywatchers produced the *Makoċe Wiċaŋḣpi Wowapi* — the D(L)akota
Star Map — as a companion to its Ojibwe counterpart, with an accompanying
constellation guidebook published in 2014. Lee (2016) records **Jim Rock**
as the language expert for the D(L)akota map, working with elders from the
South Dakota area, and names **Charlene O'Rourke** among the guidebook's
authors.

"D(L)akota" is written with the bracketed L because the same words differ
between the Dakota and Lakota dialects; the sources give both forms
throughout, and both are kept here.

The figures are organised by season — Waniyetu (winter), Ptaŋyetu (fall),
Bdoketu/Bloketu (summer), Wetu (spring) — with the northern figures listed
apart.

Much of this material is ceremonial. *Tayamni*, the buffalo in three parts,
*Inipi*, the sweat lodge, *Gleṡka Wakaŋ*, the sacred hoop, and *Oċeti
Ṡakowiŋ*, the seven sacred rites, are not merely star patterns. **The
teachings are not retold here.** Only the names and their translations are
recorded, and readers who want the teachings should go to the published
guidebook and, as the authors themselves urge, to elders and community
members.

## Constellations

**No line geometry is published in the open-access source.** Appendix B of
Lee (2016) gives each figure's name, its translation, and which Greek
constellation or asterism it overlaps — but the star-by-star shapes exist
only in the star-map artwork, which is copyrighted and not reproduced here.
Drawing an outline from a Greek-constellation cross-reference would be an
invention, so **none is drawn**. These figures appear by name, without
lines, until geometry can be obtained with permission.

The cross-references below are the authors telling an English-reading
audience roughly where to look. They are **not** part of the D(L)akota name.

Names the source gives to a **single star or cluster** are attached to that
object rather than carried as a figure: Tayamni siŋte to Sirius, the three
Arcturus names to Arcturus, Wiċaŋḣpi Waziyata and Wiċaŋḣpi Owaŋjila to
Polaris, and Wiċiŋyaŋna Ṡakowiŋ and Tayamni pa to the Pleiades. A figure's
name belongs to the figure, not to each of its stars.

Note that the Big Dipper carries **six** distinct D(L)akota names in the
source, several of them naming only a part of it. They are recorded as
separate entries because the source records them separately.

The source prints "Columbia" for the Snake's cross-reference; that is read
here as **Columba**, the constellation, which is the only reading that makes
sense beside Puppis and Canis Major. It is the one place where the source's
text has been corrected rather than copied.

**Orthography** follows the source as printed, including its own variant
spellings. It has not been reviewed by a speaker for this project, and
corrections from speakers are welcome.

## Authors

The star knowledge is D(L)akota. It is held by D(L)akota people and
communities, not by this project and not by any individual researcher.

The map and guidebook credited in the sources: **Annette S. Lee**, **Jim
Rock** and **Charlene O'Rourke**, under the Native Skywatchers initiative
founded by Annette S. Lee, working with elders from the South Dakota area.

This sky culture file was assembled for Indigenous Stellarium from the
open-access papers listed above. Any error in transcription is ours, not the
authors'.
