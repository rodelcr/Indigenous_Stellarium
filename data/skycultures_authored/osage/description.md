# Osage (Wazhazhe)

> **DRAFT — NOT FOR PUBLICATION.** This entry is derived from a published
> ethnographic source. It has **not** been reviewed or approved by the Osage
> Nation for use in this application, and it is excluded from every public

## Introduction

The Osage (Wazhazhe) sky beings recorded here come from the child-naming
rite, in which the people of a gens address gods and goddesses of the sky
and ask permission to take "bodies" from them for their children. The sky
figures are not decorative: they are addressed as relatives — grandfather
and grandmother — and paired by sex.

This entry records **two versions**, from two different gentes, because the
source records two and they do not agree. Flattening them into one list
would erase what the source is about.

## Description

**Version recorded from Shoⁿ´-ge-moⁿ-iⁿ**, a Noⁿ´-hoⁿ-zhiⁿ-ga of the
Ṭsi´-zhu Wa-shta-ge gens — four gods and four goddesses:

| | Osage | Identified as | Addressed as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wa-ḳoⁿ´-da Hoⁿ´-ba doⁿ | God of Day (the Sun) | grandfather |
| 2 | Wa-ḳoⁿ´-da Hoⁿ doⁿ | Goddess of Night (the Moon) | grandmother |
| 3 | Wa´-ṭse-do-ga | Male Star (the Morning star) | grandfather |
| 4 | Wa´-ṭse Mi-ga | Female Star (the Evening star) | grandmother |
| 5 | Wa´-ba-ha | Litter (the Dipper) | grandfather |
| 6 | Ṭa-p̣a´ | Deer-head (the Pleiades) | **grandmother** |
| 7 | Ṭa Tha´-bthiⁿ | Three-deer (Orion's Belt) | **grandfather** |
| 8 | Mi-ḳa-ḳ'e u-ḳi-tha-ç'iⁿ | Stars-strung-together (theta and iota in Orion) | grandmother |

**Version recorded from Xu´-tha-wa-ṭoⁿ-iⁿ** of the Ṭsi´-zhu Wa-noⁿ (Elder
Ṭsi´-zhu) gens — six gods and four goddesses:

| | Osage | Identified as | Addressed as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wa-ḳoⁿ´-da Hoⁿ´-ba doⁿ | God of Day (the Sun) | grandfather |
| 2 | Wa-ḳoⁿ´-da Hoⁿ doⁿ | Goddess of Night (the Moon) | grandmother |
| 3 | Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e Hoⁿ-baⁿ doⁿ | Day-star (Morning star) | grandfather |
| 4 | Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e Hoⁿ´ doⁿ | Night-star (Evening star) | grandmother |
| 5 | Wa´-ba-ha | Litter, the Dipper (Great Bear) | grandfather |
| 6 | Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e u-ḳi-tha-ç'iⁿ | Double-star | grandmother |
| 7 | Ṭa-p̣a´ | Deer-head, Pleiades | **grandfather** |
| 8 | Ṭa Tha´-bthiⁿ | Three-deer, Orion's Belt | **grandmother** |
| 9 | Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e Zhu-dse | Red-star, the Pole star | grandfather |
| 10 | Shoⁿ-ge A-ga-k'e e-goⁿ | Dog-star, Sirius | grandfather |

**The two versions swap the kinship of the Pleiades and Orion's Belt.** In
the Wa-shta-ge version Deer-head is grandmother and Three-deer grandfather;
in the Wa-noⁿ version it is the reverse. This is recorded, not reconciled.

The original publication carries two charts drawn for these figures —
Fig. 3, the constellation Wa´-ba-ha (Ursa Major), and Fig. 4, Ṭa Tha´-bthiⁿ,
Three Deer (in Orion). **Neither is reproduced here.**

### A refusal recorded in the source

Xu´-tha-wa-ṭoⁿ-iⁿ, asked for the child-naming ritual of his gens,
**declined to give it in full**, and consented only "with some reluctance"
to recite its first wi´-gi-e. That refusal is part of the record, and it is
the reason this entry is a draft rather than a publication: an Osage
authority in 1928 set a limit on what he would have recorded, and nothing
about this material arriving in a public planetarium a century later
dissolves that.

## Constellations

##### Wa´-ba-ha — Litter (the Dipper / Great Bear)
Ursa Major. Addressed as grandfather in both versions. La Flesche's
dictionary glosses `wa'-ba-ha` as "the stretcher; the Great Dipper", and
notes the constellation "is frequently referred to in ceremonial rituals by
the Osage."

##### Ṭa-p̣a´ — Deer-head (the Pleiades)
The Pleiades. Grandmother in the Wa-shta-ge version, grandfather in the
Wa-noⁿ version. A subgens of the Hoⁿ Zho´-i-ga-the gens is named Ṭa-p̣a´
Zho-i-ga-the, "Deer-head or Pleiades People".

##### Ṭa Tha´-bthiⁿ — Three-deer (Orion's Belt)
"The three great stars that form Orion's Belt." Grandfather in the
Wa-shta-ge version, grandmother in the Wa-noⁿ version.

##### Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e u-ḳi-tha-ç'iⁿ — Stars-strung-together / Double-star
Identified in the source as theta and iota in Orion. Addressed as
grandmother in both versions.

### Star terms recorded in the dictionary but not part of the pairings above

`mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e` star · `mi-ka'-k'e siⁿ-dse stse-dse` long-tailed star, comet ·
`mi-ka'-k'e u-xpa-the` meteor, falling star — "the year 1833 was known as
the year of falling stars" · `mi-ka'-k'e u-zhoⁿ-ge` the path of a star,
orbit · `mi-ka'-k'e mon-thiⁿ a-zhi` "star-moving-not", the Pole star ·
`Mi-ka'-k'e-zhiⁿ-ga` Little-star, a personal name.

Note a tension inside the source: both `mi-ka'-k'e moⁿ-thiⁿ a-zhi` and
`mi-ka'-k'e zhu-dse` are glossed as the Pole star. Recorded as found.

## License

**Undetermined. Withheld from publication pending Osage Nation review.**

The sources are out of copyright. That is not the question this project
asks. This is ceremonial knowledge, recorded from named Osage authorities by
an outside ethnographer for a federal bureau, and one of those authorities
declined to give his ritual in full. Copyright expiry does not convert that
into material a planetarium may publish.

The Osage Nation Language Department has authorised a **separate** project
on Osage language preservation. That authorisation covers that work. It does
not extend to publishing Osage ceremonial sky knowledge in a public
application, and treating it as though it did would be exactly the
substitution this project exists to refuse.

No terms are asserted here on the Osage Nation's behalf.
