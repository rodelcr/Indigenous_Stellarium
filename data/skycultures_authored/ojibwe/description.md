# Ojibwe (Anishinaabe)

## Introduction

Ojibwe names for constellations and stars, as published in the open-access
scholarly record of the **Native Skywatchers** initiative — a revitalization
programme founded by Dr. Annette S. Lee, working with Ojibwe language
experts, artists, elders and educators.

What is recorded here is **names and their translations only**. No outlines
are drawn, and no artwork is reproduced. See *Constellations* below for why.

## Description

Native Skywatchers produced the *Ojibwe Giizhig Anung Masinaaigan* — the
Ojibwe Sky Star Map — first shared with regional educators at a teachers'
workshop in June 2012, together with an accompanying constellation guide.
The map is a mixed-media painting: William Wilson painted the Ojibwe
constellations in the woodland x-ray style, following a star chart by Carl
Gawboy, with the remaining artwork by Annette Lee. Jeff Tibbetts co-wrote
the guide. William Wilson, a first-language speaker, led the compilation of
the star vocabulary, relying on the Baraga (1992) and Nichols & Nyholm
(1995) dictionaries.

The figures are organised by season — Dagwaagin (fall), Biboon (winter),
Ziigwan (spring), Niibin (summer) — with the circumpolar figures listed
apart, since from mid-northern latitudes they never set.

Some of this knowledge is tied to ceremony, and the sources say so plainly.
Lee (2013) records that Nanaboujou stories are traditionally told only when
there is snow on the ground. **The teachings and stories are not retold
here.** Only the names and their translations are recorded, and readers who
want the teachings should go to the published guidebooks and, as the authors
themselves urge, to elders and community members.

## Constellations

**No line geometry is published in the open-access sources.** Both papers
give each figure's name, its translation, and which Greek constellation it
overlaps — but the star-by-star shapes exist only in the star-map artwork,
which is copyrighted and not reproduced here. Drawing an outline from a
Greek-constellation cross-reference would be an invention, so **none is
drawn**. These figures appear by name, without lines, until geometry can be
obtained with permission.

The Greek cross-references below are the authors telling an English-reading
audience roughly where to look. They are **not** part of the Ojibwe name.

| Ojibwe | English | Season | Where to look |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mooz | Moose | Dagwaagin (fall) | Pegasus, Lacerta |
| Bugonagiizhig | Hole in the Sky | Dagwaagin (fall) | Pleiades |
| Madoo'asinik | Sweating Stones | Dagwaagin (fall) | Pleiades |
| Biboonkeonini | Wintermaker | Biboon (winter) | Orion, Canis Minor, Taurus |
| Mishi bizhiw | Curly Tail, Great Panther | Ziigwan (spring) | Leo, Hydra |
| Madoodiswan | Sweat Lodge | Ziigwan (spring) | Corona |
| Ajiijaak / Bineshi Okanin | Crane / Skeleton Bird | Niibin (summer) | Cygnus |
| Noondeshin Bemaadizid | Exhausted Bather | Niibin (summer) | Hercules |
| Nanaboujou | Nanaboujou | Niibin (summer) | Scorpio |
| Maang | Loon | Giwedinang (north) | Little Dipper / Ursa Minor |
| Ojiig | Fischer | Giwedinang (north) | Big Dipper / Ursa Major |
| Giwedin'anung | North Star | Giwedinang (north) | Polaris |

Bugonagiizhig and Madoo'asinik are two names for the Pleiades; both are
recorded against that cluster rather than as separate figures.
Giwedin'anung is recorded against Polaris.

**One discrepancy is left visible rather than resolved.** For Mishi bizhiw,
the body text of Lee (2013) reads "Leo, Cancer" while its own Table 1 — and
Table A1 of Lee (2016) — read "Leo, Hydra". The tables are followed here,
and the disagreement is noted rather than silently decided.

**Orthography** follows the sources as printed. It has not been reviewed by
a speaker for this project, and corrections from speakers are welcome.

## Authors

The star knowledge is Ojibwe. It is held by Ojibwe people and communities,
not by this project and not by any individual researcher.

The map and guide credited in the sources: **Annette S. Lee**, **William
Wilson**, **Carl Gawboy** and **Jeff Tibbetts**, under the Native
Skywatchers initiative founded by Annette S. Lee. The sources further
acknowledge Carl Gawboy's forty years of research, the collaborator **Paul
Schultz** (White Earth, d. 2011), and the elders who kept this knowledge
alive.

This sky culture file was assembled for Indigenous Stellarium from the two
open-access papers listed above. Any error in transcription is ours, not the
authors'.
