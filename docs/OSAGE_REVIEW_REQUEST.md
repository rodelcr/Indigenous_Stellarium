# Osage sky knowledge in Indigenous Stellarium — request for review

**Prepared for:** the Osage Nation Language Department
**Prepared by:** Rodrigo Córdova Rosado, Center for Astrophysics | Harvard &
Smithsonian
**Date:** 2026-09-03
**The material described here is already published** at
<https://rodelcr.github.io/Indigenous_Stellarium/> and can be removed
immediately on request. Nothing in this document assumes an answer.

---

## What the project is

Indigenous Stellarium is a planetarium in a web browser where indigenous
constellations are what you see by default, and the Western/IAU constellations
are switched off unless a viewer asks for them. It is currently a
demonstration, built to be shown to communities rather than to collect
anything from them: there are no accounts, no submissions, and no review
process yet.

It is free software, and the sky-culture data it uses is separate from the
software's licence. Each community's material stays under whatever terms that
community sets. The project asserts none on anyone's behalf.

## What is currently published as Osage

Everything below is on the public site today. It is listed in full, because a
request for review should not describe less than is actually there.

**Fourteen names, with their meanings,** from the page's own table:

| Osage | What the name means | Where La Flesche says it is |
|---|---|---|
| Wa´-ba-ha | Litter, the stretcher | the Dipper; also given as the Great Bear, and the chart is captioned Ursa Major |
| Ṭa-p̣a´ | Deer-head | the Pleiades |
| Ṭa Tha´-bthiⁿ | Three-deer | the three great stars of Orion's Belt |
| Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e u-ḳi-tha-ç'iⁿ | Stars-strung-together, or Double-star | theta and iota in Orion |
| Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e Zhu-dse | Red-star | the Pole star |
| Shoⁿ´-ge a-ga-ḳ'e e-goⁿ | Dog that lies suspended in the sky | Sirius, in Canis Major |
| Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e Hoⁿ-baⁿ doⁿ | Day-star | the morning star |
| Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e Hoⁿ´ doⁿ | Night-star | the evening star |
| Wa´-ṭse-do-ga | Male Star | the morning star |
| Wa´-ṭse Mi-ga | Female Star | the evening star |
| Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e toⁿ-ga | The great star | the morning star |
| Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e moⁿ-thiⁿ a-zhi | "Star-moving-not" | the Pole star |
| Wa-ḳoⁿ´-da Hoⁿ´-ba doⁿ | God of Day | the Sun |
| Wa-ḳoⁿ´-da Hoⁿ doⁿ | Goddess of Night | the Moon |

Six of these are attached to the sky itself. Ṭa-p̣a´ (the Pleiades),
Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e Zhu-dse (Polaris) and Shoⁿ´-ge a-ga-ḳ'e e-goⁿ (Sirius) are shown
when a viewer clicks that star or cluster. Ṭa Tha´-bthiⁿ and
Mi-ḳa´-ḳ'e u-ḳi-tha-ç'iⁿ are drawn as figures. Wa´-ba-ha is listed as a
figure but not drawn. The other eight appear only on the culture's
description page. The morning
and evening stars are named as such in the sources; no planet is identified
and none is assumed.

## What is deliberately not published

- **The wi´-gi-e are not reproduced.** None of the recitations appear, in
  Osage or in translation. The word wi´-gi-e occurs once, in a sentence
  saying they are excluded.
- **La Flesche's two charts are not reproduced** — his drawing of Wa´-ba-ha
  and his drawing of Ṭa Tha´-bthiⁿ.
- **No figure is drawn whose shape the text does not state.** Two are drawn,
  because the sources say which stars make them up: Three-deer is the three
  belt stars, and Stars-strung-together is theta and iota. Wa´-ba-ha is not
  drawn. Knowing it is the Dipper does not tell us the shape of a litter, and
  the only source that would is the chart we are not reproducing.
- **Nothing is invented.** No name, meaning, star, or story has been supplied
  from anywhere but the two published volumes below.

## Where it comes from

- Francis La Flesche, *The Osage Tribe: Two Versions of the Child-Naming
  Rite*, Forty-third Annual Report of the Bureau of American Ethnology,
  1925–1926 (Washington: Government Printing Office, 1928), pp. 23–164.
- Francis La Flesche, *A Dictionary of the Osage Language*, Bureau of
  American Ethnology Bulletin 109 (Washington: Government Printing Office,
  1932).

Both are United States government publications and are out of copyright.
**We do not treat that as permission.** Copyright expiry says what the law
allows; it does not say what the Osage Nation wants, and the second is the
question this document is asking.

Two things about the source shape the way it has been handled:

1. It records ceremonial material. The 1928 volume is a ritual, and the
   dictionary notes that Wa´-ba-ha "is frequently referred to in ceremonial
   rituals by the Osage."
2. **It records a refusal.** Xu´-tha-wa-ṭoⁿ-iⁿ of the Ṭsi´-zhu Wa-noⁿ gens,
   asked for the child-naming ritual of his gens, "declined to give it in
   full," and consented only "with some reluctance" to recite its first
   wi´-gi-e. An Osage authority in the 1920s set a limit on what he would
   have recorded. We have tried to treat that limit as still standing.

## What we are asking

Any of the following, and we will act on it without argument:

1. **Remove it.** Say so and it comes down. No justification needed.
2. **Correct it.** Orthography, translations, or anything we have
   misunderstood. La Flesche's spellings are from the 1920s and are not the
   Nation's current orthography; we would rather carry yours.
3. **Restrict it.** If some of these names should not be public, or should be
   shown only in some contexts, tell us which. The software is being built so
   that "restricted" and "withheld" are real states, not absences.
4. **Set the terms.** What licence, if any, should this material carry? At
   present the entry reads "to be determined by the Osage Nation," and no
   terms are asserted.
5. **Say who should be credited, and how.**

We are not asking for permission to add more. If the Nation would like more
of the recorded sky material included, that would be a separate conversation
begun by you, not by us.

## On the relationship to the language project

The Osage Nation Language Department has approved a separate project of
Rodrigo's on Osage language preservation, including programmatic access to
the online dictionary. **That approval was not treated as covering this.**
Publishing sky knowledge in a public planetarium is a different act with a
different audience, and reading one authorisation as covering the other is
exactly the substitution this project is trying not to make. This is a fresh
request.

## Practical notes

- Removal takes minutes and requires no coordination on your side.
- The entry can be replaced with a placeholder reading "Osage (Wazhazhe)" and
  an invitation to contribute, which is how communities with no dataset are
  shown — Rapa Nui, Mapuche, Diaguita and Quechua all appear that way now.
- The project keeps a governance document describing a steward model, in which
  designated members of a community hold the decision over their own entries.
  It is a draft and has been ratified by nobody. If the Nation has views on
  how that should work, they would carry more weight than anything in it now.
